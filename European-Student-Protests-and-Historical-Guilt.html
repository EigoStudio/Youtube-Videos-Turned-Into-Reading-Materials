Part 1: The Spreading Wave of Student Protests Across European Campuses

In recent months, a wave of fervent student activism has swept across prestigious universities throughout Western Europe. What began as isolated sit-ins at renowned institutions like Sciences Po and the Sorbonne in Paris swiftly metastasized into coordinated demonstrations across the United Kingdom, Germany, the Netherlands, and Switzerland. Campus quadrangles have been transformed into encampments, with demonstrators establishing barricades, chanting incendiary slogans, and clashing with municipal riot police. While international commentators initially hypothesized that these volatile demonstrations were driven predominantly by newly arrived immigrant communities or second-generation diasporas, on-the-ground observations revealed a far more startling reality.

A substantial proportion—if not the overwhelming majority—of the most impassioned participants in these university occupations are native white, affluent European youths. Born into generational privilege and enrolled in elite academic faculties, these young intellectuals have willingly placed themselves on the ideological vanguard. Rather than merely demonstrating solidarity with geopolitical causes, they exhibit a level of radicalism that frequently outpaces the communities they claim to advocate for. They aggressively demand that university administrations divest from international partnerships and systematically repudiate Western diplomatic alliances.

This sudden surge in aggressive student mobilization cannot be explained merely as a spontaneous reaction to foreign conflicts. Instead, it reflects an underlying cultural fracture simmering within European societies. The presence of indigenous middle-class students leading these civil disruptions indicates that a profound psychological transformation has taken hold in modern Western academia. To comprehend why young people who enjoy unprecedented freedom and prosperity are fiercely repudiating their own societal structures, one must investigate the ideological indoctrination that defines contemporary humanities education.

【Part 2 学習画面】

Part 2: The Ideological Engine: White Guilt and Masochistic Pedagogy

The psychological catalyst propelling native European students toward self-directed hostility is a phenomenon sociological analysts identify as "White Guilt." Over the past several decades, European primary and secondary educational curricula have undergone a radical revisionist shift. History lessons no longer emphasize the Enlightenment, scientific innovation, or the democratic institutional advancements pioneered by European civilization. Instead, modern syllabi dwell disproportionately on the historical transgressions of the West—namely transatlantic slavery, imperial subjugation, economic exploitation, and colonial violence.

This relentless pedagogical focus on national transgressions inculcates a pervasive sense of hereditary culpability among impressionable pupils. From an early age, students are conditioned to perceive Western identity not as an inheritance of legal equality and cultural achievement, but as an enduring badge of historic villainy. Consequently, young Europeans are systematically deprived of healthy civic pride. Deprived of a positive cultural anchor, this suppressed self-esteem manifests as existential guilt, convincing many that their affluent circumstances are illegitimate and built entirely upon the subjugation of the Global South.

In this intellectual milieu, joining extreme protest movements becomes a quasi-religious ritual of moral atonement. By shouting down Western institutions, occupying administrative buildings, and siding unconditionally with anti-Western causes, these students seek to cleanse themselves of ancestral shame. Their radicalism is less about pragmatic foreign policy than it is an urgent therapeutic mechanism to perform repentance in public. They project their internal, self-deprecating neuroses onto geopolitical controversies, viewing their own civilization as uniquely corrupt and worthy of dismantling.

【Part 3 学習画面】

Part 3: Reclaiming Historical Balance: Toward Critical and Constructive Patriotism

A sustainable and healthy democratic society cannot endure when rooted in unmitigated national self-loathing. While uncritical chauvinism—blind nationalism that glorifies every historical act—is inherently dangerous, excessive historical masochism is equally corrosive to social cohesion. True intellectual maturity requires cultivating what political philosophers designate as "critical patriotism": a balanced perspective that acknowledges historical mistakes with intellectual honesty while vigorously upholding the enduring institutional virtues of one’s nation.

Educators and academic institutions must reject simplistic moral binary frameworks that reduce human history to crude categories of villainous oppressors and blameless victims. History is an evolutionary chronicle of universal human struggle, where every civilization across the globe has engaged in territorial conquest and moral failures. What distinguishes modern constitutional democracies is not an absence of historical sins, but their unique capacity for self-correction. The ideals of individual liberty, human rights, representative governance, and the rule of law were forged through arduous debate within the Western tradition, providing the very conceptual tools used today to combat injustice.

By contextualizing historical errors within the standards of their contemporary eras rather than judging them solely through retrospective 21st-century lenses, school education can foster resilience rather than paralysis. Instilling young people with a legitimate appreciation for the hard-won achievements of their forebears empowers them to contribute constructively to the future. A nation that completely loses confidence in its own foundational legitimacy forfeits the moral resolve required to defend its democratic institutions against authoritarian challenges.

【Part 4 学習画面】

Part 4: Postwar Parallels: Japan's WGIP Legacy and the Challenge of Jigyaku-shikan

The psychological malaise observed among contemporary European youths bears a striking structural resemblance to the postwar educational experience of Japan. Following its defeat in 1945, Japan was placed under the authority of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers (GHQ). To ensure that Japan would never again pose a military threat to Western powers, GHQ instituted comprehensive ideological reorientation initiatives, most notably the War Guilt Information Program (WGIP). Media narratives, public broadcasting, and academic textbooks were rigorously censored and rewritten to impress upon the Japanese populace that their nation had been an unmitigated militaristic aggressor solely responsible for the catastrophic Pacific conflict.

This institutionalized re-education gave birth to what Japanese intellectuals term Jigyaku-shikan (a masochistic or self-deprecating view of national history). For subsequent generations of postwar students, school curricula overwhelmingly focused on wartime atrocities, colonial misdeeds, and unconditional submission to pacifist idealism, while systematically obscuring Japan's rich cultural continuity, modernization triumphs, and complex geopolitical security dilemmas during the Meiji and Showa periods. Similar to Western pupils afflicted with White Guilt, postwar Japanese youths were socialized to equate legitimate national pride with dangerous militarism, rendering public expressions of patriotism culturally taboo.

The consequence of this enduring educational legacy is a noticeable erosion of civic self-confidence and strategic resolve in the face of modern international tensions. When an entire generation is conditioned to believe that their national heritage is fundamentally flawed, collective defense, sovereign dignity, and diplomatic determination are gravely weakened. By examining the crisis of self-deprecating history currently paralyzing Western Europe, Japanese learners gain a crucial mirror to re-examine their own postwar narratives, discovering that overcoming inherited collective guilt is an indispensable prerequisite for securing independent national survival.
